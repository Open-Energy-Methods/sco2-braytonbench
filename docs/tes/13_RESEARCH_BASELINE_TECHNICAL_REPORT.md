## A. Research question, geometry, governing equations

Can a deliberately bounded first-principles packed-bed model conservatively represent charge/hold/reverse discharge and report temperature-quality service independently from gross recovered heat, and can its synthetic output be emulated with a leakage-controlled surrogate without disguising extrapolation failures?

With the total bulk cross-section A, porosity epsilon, position x along the bed, and volumetric interphase coefficient Hv per *bulk* volume:

\[
\epsilon\rho_f c_f \frac{\partial T_f}{\partial t}
+\frac{\dot m c_f}{A}\frac{\partial T_f}{\partial x}
=H_v(T_s-T_f),
\]

\[
(1-\epsilon)\rho_s c_s\frac{\partial T_s}{\partial t}
=H_v(T_f-T_s).
\]

For negative flow, the upstream side reverses; the solid does not reverse its physical coordinate. There is no axial conduction, radial temperature distribution, pressure-loss work, wall heat leakage, variable properties, radiation, flow maldistribution, kinetic energy or chemistry. Physical positive density/cp, 0<epsilon<1, Hv>=0, and specified initial and stream boundary temperatures are required. Sensible energy is a convenient synthetic model, not a prescription for hot CO₂ equipment design.

The finite-volume cell volume V=A L/N yields capacities C_f=epsilon*rho_f*c_f*V and C_s=(1-epsilon)*rho_s*c_s*V and conductance H=Hv*V. The upwind cell flux is |mdot|*cp_f*(T_up-Tf). These fields are integrated using SSPRK3 with adaptive stability-bound subdivision; both fluid and solid states persist from one stage to the next.

## B. Conservation identity and stability

The full fluid-plus-solid sensible energy, at any fixed reference temperature T_ref, is

\[
U=\sum_{i=1}^N \left[C_{f,i}(T_{f,i}-T_{ref})+
C_{s,i}(T_{s,i}-T_{ref})\right].
\]

Because interphase terms cancel and internal upwind fluxes telescope, the **exact model identity** is

\[
\frac{dU}{dt}=|\dot m|c_{p,f}(T_{in}-T_{out}).
\]

SSPRK3 integrates the right-hand-side boundary flux with the same 1/6, 1/6 and 2/3 stage weights as the state. Thus good boundary/inventory agreement is *structurally expected*, **not** proof that the constitutive closure accurately predicts a measured bed.

With advection rate a=|mdot| cp_f/C_f, fluid exchange rate h_f=H/C_f, and solid rate h_s=H/C_s, the step limiter uses delta_t <= 0.75/max(a+h_f,h_s). It is a convex positivity/stability precaution, not a truncation-error guarantee.

The added candidate checks independently verify (i) affine temperature equivariance T -> aT+b for positive scales under forward, reverse and zero flow; (ii) cross-sectional/flow extensive scaling A -> qA and mdot -> q*mdot, reproducing intensive temperature fields and scaling boundary heat and both phase capacities by q; (iii) direct independent cell-energy reconstruction; and (iv) temperature-grade threshold changes cannot affect first-law trajectories. These tests constrain the code to the actual constant-property PDE; they **do not** add missing physics.

## C. Independent analytical verification

The Schumann inlet-step reference is independent of the finite-volume solver: the continuous-PDE solution is evaluated via a convergent Poisson-comparison representation of the traditional transform/Bessel solution. Initial fluid and solid profiles are uniform 350 K, inlet steps to 650 K, area 1 m², length 1 m, porosity 0.4, synthetic fluid rho 1000 kg/m³ and cp 1000 J/(kg K), solid rho 2600 kg/m³ and cp 800 J/(kg K), Hv=80,000 W/(m³ K), flow 40 kg/s.

The executable benchmark compares finite-volume outlet and midpoint solid temperatures against the independent continuum field at t=30 s for N=16,32,64,128. The documented independently calculated semi-discrete check gave approximate outlet abs errors **2.6845, 1.3442, 0.6719, 0.3358 K**, and centre-solid abs errors **3.9273, 2.1177, 1.0987, 0.5595 K**, respectively. These numbers are the *separately reported mathematical check*, **not** claims of a new experiment or necessarily bitwise values from the committed SSPRK3 benchmark. Its authoritative generated grid/step CSV and checksum manifest are produced by scripts/tes_schumann_convergence.py.

A distinct temporal study at N=64 refines maximum RK substeps from 0.2 to 0.025 s. The right-moving initial discontinuity means uniform pointwise convergence at exactly the moving front is not assured; convergence claims apply to the specified measurement points and conditions.

Sources: Schumann (1929), doi:10.1016/S0016-0032(29)91186-8; Avallone (2018) institutional thesis (see 01_LITERATURE_AND_MODEL_CONTRACT.md for bibliographic scope and limits). The original Schumann complete paper and facility datasets were **not** fully recovered or independently recited in the existing literature review; no source figures were copied.

## D. Charge, no-flow hold, reverse discharge

A prescribed stage has a mode, physical signed flow, inlet temperature and duration. Zero throughflow permits internal fluid/solid equilibration without boundary mass enthalpy. Reverse discharge uses the opposite side of the same bed; no temperature profile resets between stages.

The global inventory identity is E_charge-E_withdrawn=Delta U_bed. Total phase capacities and actual charged energy are distinct quantities. **Gross round-trip efficiency is null unless the entire fluid+solid cell temperature field returns to its initial state within the specified tolerance** and the sequence includes positive charge and recovery. This prevents an incomplete charge/discharge example from being reported as a measured efficiency. The example and tests live in examples/tes_charge_discharge.py and tests/test_tes_cycles.py.

## E. TES-to-Brayton boundary and dimensional separation

The TES-v0.5 adapter advances an **open, externally prescribed** heat-transfer-fluid stream through the packed bed, then supplies its *final outlet temperature* to the existing finite-source gas-to-CO₂ heater and **quasi-steady** recuperated Brayton state.

HTF inlet -> TES thermal inventory -> finite-source heater -> external HTF outlet.

Bed interval enthalpy/inventory change is in **J**; the final-point heater, cooler, shaft work and storage inventory rates are in **W**. They represent different time bases and are *not added as if both were concurrent interval totals*. The endpoint ledger uses exactly one physically linked source stream:

\[
|\dot m|c_{p,f}(T_{in}-T_{heater,out})
=\dot U_{bed,end}+Q_{cooler,end}+W_{shaft,end},
\]

under the adapter's final-state quasi-steady assumptions. Its finite-source CO₂ heater output must agree with the cycle's heater power. IdealGasFixture and pinned CoolPropCO2 smoke demonstrate **software compatibility** at separately feasible synthetic state points, not pressure/mass inventory dynamics, a closed HTF recirculation loop, actual generator output or a facility experiment. See docs/tes/06_SINGLE_PASS_CO2_COUPLING.md.

## F. Capacity, reversible thermal exergy and thermocline

Design solid and fluid sensible capacities are **separate** C_s,total*(T_hot-T_cold) and C_f,total*(T_hot-T_cold). Solid SOC tracks the unmodified weighted solid inventory fraction, which may be outside [0,1] if physical temperatures lie outside design reference bounds. It is not silently clipped.

For a single constant-cp phase with total heat capacity C and explicitly declared ambient temperature T_a, **thermal** exergy is

\[
B=C[(T-T_a)-T_a\ln(T/T_a)].
\]

This is a reversible thermal availability contribution, not a pressure-inclusive CO₂ exergy budget or delivered shaft work. The synthetic 10–90% thermocline width is obtained by interpolating crossing coordinates in the solid temperature profile; incomplete fronts or nonmonotonic profiles return null, not zero. Independently added tests cross-check the closed-form exergy and invariance of the interpolated width under positive affine temperature scaling when cold/hot references transform with the profile.

## G. Frozen split synthetic output surrogate — original negative OOD result preserved

TES-v0.6 generates **512 complete trajectories** with 16 samples each: 256 train, 64 validation, 128 sealed in-domain test and 64 intentionally out-of-domain cases, a total of 8192 samples. Whole-trajectory separation, source-file SHA256s, three predeclared boosting candidates and freeze-before-evaluation prevent tuning on correlated test time rows.

TES-v0.7 selected its *balanced* HistGradientBoostingRegressor pair using training/validation only. In-domain 128-run test: surrogate outlet RMSE **6.1965 K** and solid SOC RMSE **0.011619**, compared to 15.0918 K and 0.036980 from a 2-cell physics baseline, and 151.9444 K and 0.509882 from persistence. **All six predefined in-domain criteria met**.

But on the separately shifted 64-run OOD set, the surrogate outlet RMSE **14.2522 K**, SOC RMSE **0.028423**, while the simpler two-cell conservative solver obtains **6.5965 K** and **0.014768**. This is a **negative extrapolation result**, not an acceptable surrogate generalization claim. **16 raw normalized bound violations** were counted before explicit clipping on the in-domain evaluation. Clipping does not establish raw physical admissibility.

The surrogate learns *outlet temperature and solid SOC*, not the full fluid/solid state or a first-law-consistent boundary-integrated enthalpy flux. It is an offline schedule-conditioned output emulator, not a closed-loop controller or a conservative dynamical ROM. The TES-v0.9 installed-package toy predictor uses newly generated synthetic inputs only; it does not repeat or overwrite the sealed v0.7 holdouts. See docs/tes/08_FROZEN_SPLIT_SURROGATE.md and 09_FROZEN_ROM_RESULTS_2026-11-07.md.

## H. TES-v0.8 operating window and numerical sensitivity

The predeclared synthetic nominal packed bed is length 1 m, area 0.1 m², porosity 0.5, fluid rho 5 kg/m³ and cp 1000 J/(kg K), solid rho 1000 kg/m³ and cp 700 J/(kg K), Hv 500 W/(m³ K), inlet cold/hot 350/650 K, ambient 300 K, charge/hold/discharge 0.8/0.1/1.0 dimensionless thermal turnovers, and threshold for qualification 500 K.

A fully qualified heater service temperature is an **operational indicator**:

\[
E_{useful}(T_q)=\int_{\mathrm{discharge}}\mathbf 1[T_{out}(t)\ge T_q]
|\dot m|c_p[T_{out}(t)-T_{cold}]dt.
\]

This integral is evaluated by **piecewise-linear interpolation of actual SSPRK3 internal-substep outlet samples**. It remains separate from the exact RK-stage conservative *total recovered heat*; a useful-grade failure must **never** discard lower-grade energy from the first-law ledger.

At N=96: recovered 2.120487 MJ; useful above 500 K only **6.570557 kJ (~0.31%)**. The nominal state does not return to its original complete cell field; no round-trip ratio. The thermal-exergy metric at the charged state is approximately 1.280 MJ relative to 300 K. The thermocline-width indicator is undefined when the normalized profile lacks both complete threshold crossings.

Spatial refinement of threshold-qualified heat (kJ) is approximately:
N=6:0.175; 12:3.469; 24:5.206; 48:6.109; 96:6.571; 192:6.804. The N=96→192 qualified-heat difference is ~3.4%, while recovered-energy difference is ~0.25%. **First-law closure is not equivalent to grid convergence**; N=12 is not trustworthy for this temperature-grade measure. Internal outlet sampling stride and SSPRK3 timestep sensitivity at fixed N=96 are much smaller than this spatial discrepancy. A finer grid is not automatically exact continuum truth.

The OAT panel includes **29 scenarios** (flow, porosity, bed volume/geometry, Hv, charge/hold/discharge durations and temperature threshold). The **eight** fully crossed flow × porosity × Hv cases produce seven signed main/interaction contrasts. At the extreme levels, flow × Hv interaction on >500 K qualified heat is approximately **−1494.9 kJ**. Interactions and abrupt no-service thresholds preclude naive additive extrapolation. Flow and Hv responses and length/area responses exhibit confounding in this idealized model; no pressure-drop/wall-loss geometry physics is solved.

These are deterministic **sensitivity contrasts**, not statistical confidence intervals or distributions of parameter uncertainty. Numerical, parametric and **unquantified model-form** uncertainty classes must not be merged.

See docs/tes/10_OPERATING_WINDOW_SENSITIVITY.md and 11_OPERATING_WINDOW_RESULTS_2026-11-07.md.
